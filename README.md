# clanker-scratch

Scratch repository for Clanker Corner Slack and git-host acceptance runs.

## Recipes

### Pollo alla Cacciatora

Serves 4. About 15 minutes of prep, 50 minutes of cooking.

#### Ingredients

- 8 bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs (about 1.2 kg)
- Salt and black pepper
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 1 onion, sliced
- 1 red pepper, deseeded and cut into strips
- 3 cloves garlic, sliced
- 1 sprig rosemary
- 2 bay leaves
- 175 ml dry white wine
- 400 g crushed tomatoes (1 tin)
- 125 ml chicken stock
- 100 g black olives, pitted
- 1 tbsp red wine vinegar
- Flat-leaf parsley, chopped, to serve

#### Method

1. Pat the chicken dry and season all over with salt and pepper. Heat the
   olive oil in a large, deep frying pan or casserole over medium-high heat.
2. Brown the chicken skin-side down for 6–8 minutes without moving it, then
   turn and brown the other side for 3 minutes. Transfer to a plate. Pour off
   all but about 2 tbsp of the fat.
3. Lower the heat to medium. Add the onion and pepper and cook for 8 minutes
   until softened and lightly coloured. Add the garlic, rosemary and bay
   leaves and cook for 1 minute more.
4. Pour in the wine, scraping the base of the pan, and simmer for 2–3 minutes
   until reduced by half.
5. Stir in the crushed tomatoes and stock. Return the chicken to the pan
   skin-side up, nestling it into the sauce so the skin stays above the
   liquid. Bring to a gentle simmer.
6. Cover partially and cook for 30 minutes, turning the chicken once at the
   halfway point, until the meat pulls easily from the bone.
7. Add the olives and cook uncovered for a final 5–10 minutes to thicken the
   sauce. Stir in the vinegar, discard the rosemary and bay, and taste for
   seasoning. Scatter with parsley and serve.

#### Notes

- Chicken drumsticks or a jointed whole bird work just as well; adjust the
  covered cooking time to 35–40 minutes for larger pieces.
- Better made a day ahead. Cool, refrigerate, and reheat gently on the hob —
  the sauce deepens overnight.
- Serve with polenta, roast potatoes or plenty of bread. Rice is not
  traditional here but soaks up the sauce nicely.
